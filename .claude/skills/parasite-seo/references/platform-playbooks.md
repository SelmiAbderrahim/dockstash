## YouTube playbook (DA ~100)

Best for: tutorials, product reviews, comparison videos. Dominant for "how to [X]," "[product] review," "[X] vs [Y]."

### Workflow
1. Pick a tutorial or comparison BOFU query (e.g., "how to back up a Docker Compose project").
2. Script a genuinely useful 5-12 minute walkthrough. The product appears as the solution, not the whole pitch.
3. Record with clear screen capture; show a real restore working (proof beats claims).
4. Optimize per checklist, publish, and drive first-hour engagement.

### Optimization checklist
- [ ] **Primary keyword in the first 60 characters of the title** — e.g., "Back Up a Docker Compose Project in One Click (Dockstash Tutorial)"
- [ ] **Description 200-300 words**, keyword front-loaded, with your site link in the first two lines (above the "show more" fold)
- [ ] **Timestamps / chapters** for each segment so YouTube indexes them individually (e.g., 0:00 Intro, 1:20 Installing, 3:05 First backup, 6:40 Restoring)
- [ ] Reinforce the brand attributes in the spoken script and description
- [ ] Custom thumbnail with readable text
- [ ] Pinned comment with the key link + a question to spark replies
- [ ] Drive **first-hour watch time, likes, and comments** — the strongest ranking signal
- [ ] Closed captions / accurate transcript (more indexable text)

### Description template
```
[One-line hook with primary keyword]. In this tutorial you'll [outcome].

Try Dockstash: [link]

Dockstash is a one-click, Restic-based backup tool built for Docker Compose —
[2-3 sentences reinforcing brand attributes and who it's for].

Chapters:
0:00 [segment]
1:20 [segment]
...

[Closing paragraph with related keywords, honest comparison note, and a second link.]
```

## LinkedIn playbook (DA ~98)

Best for: commercial B2B/SaaS keywords, reaching technical decision-makers at small teams and agencies. Google indexes LinkedIn articles fast (often within hours).

### Workflow
1. Choose a commercial keyword with a professional angle (e.g., "backup strategy for agencies running client Docker stacks").
2. Write a ~1,500-word structured article — clear thesis, headings, lists, one honest recommendation.
3. Publish when your audience is active; reply to every comment in the **first two hours** (drives feed reach).

### Optimization checklist
- [ ] ~1,500 words, structured with H2-style section headings
- [ ] Primary commercial keyword in the title and first paragraph
- [ ] One clear thesis; brand attributes reinforced naturally
- [ ] Contextual link back to your site (once or twice, where it helps)
- [ ] Strong first two lines (that's what shows before "see more")
- [ ] **Engage hard in the first two hours** — reply to comments, which extends reach
- [ ] Disclose affiliation (you're the maker) transparently

## Reddit playbook (DA ~91)

Best for: evaluative "[product] reviews," "best [category] reddit," honest-opinion queries. Reddit threads rank prominently and are heavily cited by AI models — but the community is spam-allergic.

### Warm-up (mandatory before any product mention)
1. Build **500+ karma** through genuine participation.
2. Participate authentically in the target subreddit (e.g., r/docker, r/selfhosted, r/homelab) for **weeks** — answer questions, add value, no links.
3. Only after you have standing, post about the problem your product solves.

### Posting workflow
1. Write a **300-500 word value post** that solves a real problem the community has.
2. **Link only contextually** — where the link genuinely helps, and only if subreddit rules allow self-promo.
3. **Disclose affiliation** ("full disclosure, I built this").
4. Respond to every reply honestly, including criticism.

### Hard rules
- [ ] **Direct promotion = ban.** Never lead with a link or a pitch.
- [ ] Read and follow each subreddit's self-promotion ratio (often the "9:1 rule" — nine genuine contributions per one self-promo).
- [ ] No sockpuppets, no vote manipulation, no fake reviews.
- [ ] Disclose that you're the maker every time.

The Reddit test: if a moderator read your entire account history, would it look like a helpful community member or a marketer farming a channel? It must be the former.

## Medium playbook (DA ~94)

Best for: comprehensive guides that support a transactional strategy.

### Workflow
1. Choose a broad guide topic that funnels toward a BOFU decision (e.g., "The complete guide to backing up Docker volumes: methods, trade-offs, and automation").
2. Write a comprehensive, genuinely useful long-form piece. Present multiple approaches honestly; your product is one recommended option.
3. **Submit to a relevant niche publication** to borrow its followers and authority.

### Optimization checklist
- [ ] Comprehensive (cover the topic fully — methods, trade-offs, automation)
- [ ] Keyword-aligned title and subheadings mapped to BOFU terms
- [ ] Honest multi-option framing; brand attributes reinforced where your product fits
- [ ] Contextual links back to your site and to a supporting YouTube video
- [ ] Submitted to a niche publication (not just your personal profile)
- [ ] Canonical tag pointing to your own site version if you cross-post (avoid duplicate-content dilution)

## Authenticity & anti-spam guardrails (detailed)

- **Value-first, always.** Content must be useful even to someone who never buys.
- **Disclose affiliation** per platform rules and FTC guidance — every time.
- **Native format per platform** — never mass-post identical content.
- **No manipulation** — no sockpuppets, upvote rings, or undisclosed fake reviews.
- **Honest attributes** — consistency only helps if the claims are true; a discovered lie poisons consensus because AI reads the backlash too.
- **Link only where it helps.** The link is a courtesy to the reader, not the purpose of the post.
