If a client's server died tonight, could you prove their site would come back — or would you find out live, with the client on the phone?

Most agency care plans say "daily backups included." Two questions almost nobody can answer about them:

1. Is the database being dumped correctly, or is a script copying live database files that won't restore?
2. Has anyone, ever, actually restored one?

Here's the reframe that changed how I think about it: a restore test isn't an internal chore. It's a deliverable.

"Your backup was restore-tested on March 4th" is one line in a monthly client report. It turns backup from an invisible cost into visible value — something the client sees, and something you're already charging for. And the first time a client asks "how do you know?", you have a timestamped answer instead of a pause.

The agencies that get burned aren't careless. They're running 30 client stacks on tooling designed for one.
