# LI-03 — The Failed Drill (build-in-public post)

**Angle:** founder transparency · **Length:** ~180 words
**Prereq:** a real failed-drill screenshot, user data blurred (same asset as x-05).

---

The best marketing screenshot my product has ever produced is a failure report.

[image: restore-drill report, status FAILED, reason legible]

I build a backup tool, which means I sell against fear — and the honest version of that is uncomfortable. I can't promise "you'll never lose data." Nobody can, and the vendors who do are pricing in that you'll never test the claim.

What I can do is show proof. Dockstash runs scheduled restore drills: it restores your latest snapshot into a throwaway container, verifies the data, destroys the container. When a drill fails, it fails at 3am in a sandbox — not eleven months later, mid-outage, in front of your client.

So a red drill report is the system working. The failure existed either way; the drill just moved *when you find out*.

That's why I publish failure counts alongside signups when I share numbers. If I were evaluating a backup tool, the failure count is the number I'd trust.
