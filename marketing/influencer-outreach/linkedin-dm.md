# LinkedIn

LinkedIn rewards a specific opening line and punishes anything that smells like
a pitch deck. Keep it to one screen — no line breaks every sentence, no emoji
bullets.

Label convention: `li-<handle>`.

## Connection note (< 300 characters)

Sent with the invite request. You have ~300 characters including spaces; this is
289. Do **not** include the invite link here — it looks like spam and LinkedIn
may strip it.

> Hi {name} — your post on {content_reference} is the reason I'm connecting. I
> build Dockstash: Docker project backups that run a scheduled restore drill to
> prove the snapshot actually restores. No pitch, just wanted to be in the same
> room. — Selmi

Once they accept, send the DM below. Do not send it in the same breath.

## Follow-up 1 — day 3

Short. Assume the DM was buried, not rejected. Give them a reason to reply that
isn't "did you see my message".

> Hi {name} — quick one. The invite link I sent expires in ~4 weeks, and it's
> the only one I minted for you, so I'd rather it didn't quietly die.
>
> If Dockstash isn't interesting, just say so and I'll stop. If it is, the year
> starts whenever you activate it, not today.
>
> {invite_link}

## Follow-up 2 — day 8 (last one)

Close the loop and leave. The point is to be easy to remember, not hard to
escape.

> Last note from me, {name} — I'll leave you alone after this.
>
> The link stays live until {expiry_date}. If you ever want to point a restore
> drill at your own stack, it's there. If not, no hard feelings, and thanks for
> {content_reference}.
>
> {invite_link}

Then stop. Do not send a third. Do not re-add them to a sequence next quarter
without a genuinely new reason.

## What to expect

Most creators won't reply. A few will accept the connection and never open the
DM. The ones who do reply usually reply to follow-up 1, not the original, and
usually with a question about how the restore drill works. Answer that question
properly — it's the whole product.
